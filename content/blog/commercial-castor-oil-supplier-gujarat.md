## What is Commercial Castor Oil?

Commercial castor oil is the raw, unrefined vegetable oil obtained through the primary mechanical pressing of *Ricinus communis* seeds. Known across industrial manufacturing as industrial castor oil or crude expeller castor oil, this grade preserves the natural chemical profile of the castor bean. The predominant component of commercial castor oil is ricinoleic acid (12-hydroxy-cis-9-octadecenoic acid), which comprises 85% to 90% of the total triglyceride fatty acid structure.

The distinct molecular feature of ricinoleic acid is its secondary hydroxyl group situated at the 12th carbon atom. This functional group, combined with a cis double bond between carbons 9 and 10 and a carboxyl group at carbon 1, endows the oil with natural polarity, high kinematic viscosity, and wide-ranging chemical reactivity. Chemical formulators rely on this structure to execute esterification, sulfonation, and polyurethane polymerization without requiring synthetic functionalization. ## Technical Specifications & Plain Parameter Explanations

Industrial buyers evaluate commercial castor oil against established standard parameters. Understanding these parameters ensures correct selection for technical applications:

- **Free Fatty Acids (FFA)**: Specified at 2.0% maximum (calculated as oleic acid). Free fatty acids indicate the degree of hydrolytic breakdown in the seed before and during extraction. Keeping FFA below 2.0% is vital for downstream alkyd synthesis and lubricant stability.
- **Moisture and Volatile Matter**: Specified at 0.5% maximum. Residual moisture can cause haze, accelerate storage degradation, and cause uncontrolled foaming during high-temperature esterification or polyurethane urethane reactions.
- **Colour (Lovibond Scale)**: Measured in a 1-inch cell, specified at 40 units maximum (Yellow + 5xRed). The natural golden-amber coloration is standard for unbleached technical oil.
- **Hydroxyl Value**: Specified between 160 and 168 mg KOH/g. This represents the concentration of free reactive secondary hydroxyl groups, serving as the critical benchmark for isocyanate stoichiometry in polyurethane compounding.
- **Iodine Value**: Typically ranges from 82 to 90 g I2/100g, verifying the presence of a single unsaturated double bond per ricinoleic chain and confirming resistance to atmospheric oxidative rancidity.
- **soap formation Value**: Ranging between 176 and 187 mg KOH/g, providing the basis for alkali consumption in specialty soap making, sulfonation, and fatty acid cleavage reactions.

## Primary Industrial Applications

The natural fatty acid profile of commercial castor oil supports diverse chemical manufacturing sectors:

### 1. Industrial Lubricants and Heavy-Duty Greases
Due to its secondary hydroxyl group, commercial castor oil exhibits high surface affinity for metal substrates. It provides boundary lubrication films with enhanced load-carrying capacity and high shear stability in two-stroke motor oils, industrial gear lubricants, and wire rope greases.

### 2. Alkyd Resins and Industrial Coatings
In architectural coatings and maintenance enamels, commercial castor oil acts as a non-drying, plasticizing fatty component. Its hydroxyl functionality reacts into the polymer backbone, providing elasticity, adhesion to metal, and resistance to chemical corrosion.

### 3. Turkey Red Oil (Sulfonated Castor Oil)
Commercial grade oil is the industry-standard raw material for manufacturing sulfonated castor oil (Turkey Red Oil). Reacting unrefined oil with concentrated sulfuric acid creates an anionic surfactant that disperses freely in water, widely used in textile dyeing, leather tanning, and agrochemical formulations.

### 4. Bio-Based Polyurethanes and Potting Compounds
Reacting the trifunctional hydroxyl structure of commercial castor oil with polyisocyanates produces resilient polyurethane potting resins, joint sealants, and elastomeric protective liners with excellent electrical insulation.
