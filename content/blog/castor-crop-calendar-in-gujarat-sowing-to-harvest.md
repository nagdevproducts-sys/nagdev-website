## Month-by-Month Agronomic Progression: Castor Crop Calendar in Gujarat

The cultivation of *Ricinus communis* in Banaskantha and Vav-Tharad follows well-defined seasonal stages:

### July to August: Kharif Sowing and Field Preparation
- **Monsoon Arrival**: The agricultural season commences with the onset of the southwest monsoon rains in July. Farmers prepare well-drained sandy loam fields through deep plowing and harrowing.
- **Seed Planting**: High-yielding hybrid castor seeds are dibbled into moist soil at standard spacing (typically 120 cm to 150 cm between rows). The warm soil temperatures stimulate germination within seven to ten days.

### September to October: Vegetative Growth and Primary Flowering
- **Canopy Development**: Vigorous branching and deep taproot extension occur rapidly under warm, sunny conditions. Inter-cultivation and weeding maintain clean crop rows.
- **Spike Emergence**: The primary spike (the central flower raceme) emerges approximately 45 to 60 days after sowing. The monoecious flowers bear female flowers on the upper section and male flowers below, facilitating wind pollination.
- **Canal Irrigation**: As monsoon rains taper in late September, supplemental water is applied via tubewells and Narmada canal networks to support flowering.

### November to December: Spike Maturation and Secondary Flowering
- **Capsule Development**: Fertilized capsules swell and develop spiny outer hulls containing three seeds per capsule. Cooler autumn temperatures favor oil biosynthesis within the seed kernel.
- **Secondary and Tertiary Spikes**: While the primary spike ripens, the plant generates secondary and tertiary lateral branches bearing successive waves of flower spikes.

### January to April: Peak Multi-Pick Harvesting and Mandi Arrivals
- **Sequential Harvesting**: Unlike determinate crops that mature all at once, castor spikes ripen sequentially. Farmers harvest when roughly eighty percent of capsules on a spike turn brown and dry.
- **Picking Frequency**: A typical farm undergoes three to five distinct picking rounds spaced three to four weeks apart:
  - *First Picking (January)*: Harvest of the heavy primary central spikes.
  - *Second & Third Pickings (February to March)*: Harvest of secondary and tertiary lateral spikes, representing the bulk of the total crop volume.
  - *Final Picking (April to early May)*: Harvesting remaining upper spikes before summer plowing.
- **Mandi Arrivals**: Freshly threshed and sun-dried seeds flood into regional APMC mandis—including Deodar, Tharad, Bhabhar, and Dhanera—creating peak market liquidity and heavy processing volumes.

### May to June: Summer Land Preparation
- Fields are cleared of dry castor stalks (often utilized for biomass fuel), and soils are deep-plowed to solarize soil profiles before the subsequent monsoon cycle.
