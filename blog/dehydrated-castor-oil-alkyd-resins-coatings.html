Dehydrated Castor Oil & DCO-FA in High-Solids Alkyds and Coatings

Nagdev Products Technical Team
Published: 2026-09-12 · Updated: 2026-10-23
8 min read · Peer Reviewed

In the coatings and printing ink sectors, the drying capability of an oil is determined by its unsaturation profile and the presence of conjugated double bonds. Dehydrated Castor Oil (DCO) stands out among all natural and modified vegetable oils as a premier non-yellowing, rapid-drying film former.

1. The Chemistry of Thermal Dehydration

Raw castor oil is naturally a non-drying oil with an iodine value of 82–88 g I₂/100g. By heating castor oil in the presence of acidic catalysts (such as sulfuric acid, activated clays, or sulfonic acids) under vacuum at 230°C to 260°C, the hydroxyl group (-OH) at C-12 is split off with an adjacent hydrogen atom as water (H₂O).

This dehydration creates a second double bond, yielding a mixture of:

  • Conjugated 9,11-octadecadienoic acid (~30% - 35%)
  • Non-conjugated 9,12-octadecadienoic (linoleic) acid (~55% - 60%)

The resulting DCO exhibits an iodine value of 130 to 145 g I₂/100g, transforming the inert oil into a premier drying oil comparable to tung oil and dehydrated linseed oil, but without their severe yellowing tendencies.

2. Why DCO Outperforms Linseed and Soya in Architectural & Industrial Finishes

Linseed oil contains high levels of linolenic acid (with three double bonds), which causes severe dark-yellowing in white enamels and interior trim paints. In contrast, DCO contains virtually zero linolenic acid. The conjugated double bonds enable rapid through-drying via free radical polymerization rather than slow surface oxidation.

3. Key Applications of DCO & DCO Fatty Acids

  • Stoving Enamels & Appliance Finishes: Provides gloss retention, hardness, and alkali resistance during heat curing.
  • Electrical Insulating Varnishes: Superior dielectric properties and resistance to thermal degradation under prolonged operating voltage.
  • Marine and Container Primers: High water barrier properties and adhesion to ferrous substrates.
  • Printing Inks: Fast setting time and high pigment wetting capability in lithographic and flexographic formulations.

Technical FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Dehydrated Castor Oil (DCO) superior to linseed oil in white coatings? +

Linseed oil contains linolenic acid with three double bonds that oxidatively decompose into yellowing chromophores. DCO is composed primarily of conjugated 9,11- and 9,12-octadecadienoic acids, delivering rapid drying with outstanding non-yellowing film clarity under indoor and outdoor UV exposure.

What is the typical Iodine Value of Dehydrated Castor Oil? +

DCO exhibits an elevated Iodine Value of 130 to 145 g I₂/100g, reflecting the generation of conjugated double bonds after catalytic elimination of the hydroxyl group.

Which industries utilize DCO Fatty Acids (DCO-FA)? +

DCO Fatty Acid is widely used in high-solids automotive OEM finishes, can coatings, screen printing inks, and epoxy ester resins.
