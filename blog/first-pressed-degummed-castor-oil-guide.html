What is First Pressed Degummed (FPD) Castor Oil?

First Pressed Degummed (FPD) castor oil is an advanced technical vegetable oil intermediate positioned between crude commercial castor oil and fully refined FSG. Produced through the primary pressing of castor seeds, the crude oil undergoes a specialized water-degumming process designed specifically to remove naturally occurring phospholipids, gums, and mucilaginous compounds. This process leaves the natural ricinoleic acid triglyceride structure intact while eliminating the non-lipid impurities that interfere with downstream chemical synthesis.

Industrial formulators consult this first pressed degummed castor oil guide because FPD offers a cost-effective, heavy-duty alternative to fully bleached oils. In applications where pale visual appearance is secondary but thermal stability, low phosphorus content, and catalyst protection are paramount—such as bio-polyol production and urethane synthesis—FPD delivers outstanding technical performance. How Nagdev Produces FPD Castor Oil in Deodar

At Nagdev Products, manufacturing operations take place at our plant in Survey No. 301 P-2, Jada-Kotarwada Road, Village Sardarpura, Taluka Deodar, District Vav-Tharad, Gujarat 385330. With our 50 TPD seed crushing capacity, our processing line produces FPD castor oil through precision mechanical expelling and controlled water degumming:

  1. Seed Cleaning and First Pressing: High-quality castor seeds from North Gujarat's castor belt (Banaskantha and Vav-Tharad region) are cleaned and crushed in mechanical screw expellers under controlled operating temperatures. Only the initial expeller extraction is utilized for FPD production.
  2. Hydration & Conditioning: The clarified expeller oil is heated to 65°C–75°C and treated with a controlled dosage of soft, demineralized water. The water hydrates the polar headgroups of phosphatides and phospholipids, converting them from oil-soluble molecules into insoluble hydrated gums.
  3. Centrifugal Gum Separation: The oil-gum mixture passes through high-speed disc-stack centrifuges that continuously separate the dense, hydrated phosphatide sludge from the clarified oil stream.
  4. Vacuum Dehydration: The degummed oil enters a continuous vacuum drying column to flash evaporate residual water, driving the final moisture content below 0.35%.
  5. Plate-and-Frame Filtration: The dried oil passes through polishing filter presses to capture any remaining micro-particulates, yielding clear, gum-free FPD castor oil ready for bulk storage and dispatch.

Technical Specifications & Plain Parameter Explanations

Industrial chemists evaluate FPD castor oil against specific parameters that govern stability during downstream reactions:

  • Phosphorus Content: The hallmark of FPD castor oil is its low phosphorus level, typically reduced to below 20 ppm (compared to 100–300 ppm in crude oil). Removing phospholipids prevents catalyst deactivation, eliminates foaming, and stops darkening during high-temperature esterification.
  • Free Fatty Acids (FFA): Specified at 1.5% maximum (calculated as oleic acid). Lower acidity than crude oil minimizes alkali refining requirements in downstream processing.
  • Moisture and Volatile Matter: Controlled below 0.35% maximum. Keeping moisture minimal prevents hydrolysis and ensures shelf stability during bulk storage.
  • Hydroxyl Value: Maintained between 160 and 168 mg KOH/g, confirming that the secondary hydroxyl group of the ricinoleic triglyceride backbone remains entirely intact (ricinoleic acid is under development) and available for cross-linking.
  • Colour (Lovibond Scale): Measured in a 1-inch cell, typically below 25 units (Yellow + 5xRed). While darker than fully bleached FSG, FPD is significantly clearer than crude commercial oil.
  • Iodine Value: Ranging between 82 and 90 g I2/100g, verifying standard ricinoleic monounsaturation.

Primary Industrial Applications of Degummed Castor Oil

First Pressed Degummed castor oil is selected for technical processes where gum-free purity is required without the expense of full bleaching:

1. Bio-Polyurethane Polyol Synthesis

FPD is an optimal feedstock for synthesizing bio-based polyols used in rigid polyurethane insulation, structural foams, and elastomeric sealants. Because phosphatides have been removed, the oil reacts cleanly with isocyanates without generating cloudiness or fouling reaction vessels.

2. High-Temperature Industrial Lubricants

In extreme-pressure gear oils and heavy industrial lubricants, phosphatides can decompose at high operating temperatures to form sticky lacquers and deposits. FPD provides clean boundary lubrication films with enhanced thermal stability.

3. Specialty Esters and Plasticizers

Chemical manufacturers use FPD as an intermediate to produce ricinoleate esters, lubricating additives, and specialty plasticizers where low phosphorus ensures consistent reaction kinetics and prevents catalyst poisoning.

4. Alkyd Resins and Industrial Coatings

In industrial alkyd production where dark amber tinting is acceptable (such as undercoats, anti-corrosive primers, and industrial drum enamels), FPD offers excellent film flexibility and adhesion without the risk of gum precipitation during resin cooking.

Technical FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What distinguishes FPD castor oil from crude expeller oil? +

First Pressed Degummed (FPD) castor oil undergoes controlled water degumming to hydrate and extract hydratable phosphatides and phospholipids, leaving an oil with exceptionally low phosphorus content.

Why is phosphatide removal critical for chemical synthesis? +

Phosphatides and gums cause thermal discoloration, foul catalysts, and generate sediment during storage or high-temperature processing. Removing them ensures clean reaction kinetics.

What are the core technical specifications for FPD castor oil? +

FPD castor oil typically features free fatty acids under 1.5%, moisture under 0.35%, and phosphorus levels reduced below 20 ppm, alongside standard hydroxyl value of 160 to 168 mg KOH/g.
