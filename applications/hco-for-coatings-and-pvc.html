Technical Application Guide

HCO in Protective Coatings & PVC Lubrication

Hydrogenated Castor Oil (HCO, CAS #8001-78-3)—commonly known as Castor Wax—is a hard, brittle, high-melting natural triglyceride (MP 85°C–88°C). In industrial coatings, sealants, and PVC processing, it serves as a non-migrating rheological thixotrope, anti-sagging additive, and heavy-duty melt processing aid.

1. Thixotropic Activation & Rheology Control in Paints & Sealants

In solvent-borne coatings (such as marine epoxies, heavy-duty protective primers, polyurethane sealants, and road marking paints), micronized HCO operates as a temperature-activated organic thixotrope. When dispersed under high-shear Cowles dissolvers at controlled activation temperatures (typically 45°C to 65°C depending on solvent polarity), the crystalline triglyceride dissolves partially and recrystallizes upon cooling into an ultra-fine colloidal network.

This network imparts high low-shear viscosity (pseudoplasticity), preventing pigment settling during long storage and eliminating sagging or running on vertical surfaces immediately following airless spray application. Under high-shear spraying or brush application, the hydrogen-bonded network breaks down cleanly to ensure smooth flow, leveling, and minimal orange-peel defect.

2. Internal & External Lubrication in Rigid PVC Extrusion

In polymer compounding—specifically rigid PVC pipe extrusion, window profiles, and conduit manufacturing—Castor Wax functions as a high-efficiency processing aid. The balance between polar ester linkages and non-polar long C18 aliphatic chains provides dual action:

  • Internal Lubrication: Reduces intermolecular friction within the molten PVC matrix, lowering melt viscosity, reducing extruder torque, and minimizing polymer melt fracture.
  • External Lubrication & Release: Migrates moderately to the hot metal interface of extruder screws and barrel walls, preventing polymer degradation, plate-out, and die build-up, and ensuring a high-gloss surface finish.

3. Powder Coating Fluidity & Anti-Caking Performance

In electrostatic powder coatings (polyester, hybrid, and epoxy systems), atomized HCO powder acts as an effective degassing and flow control additive. Its narrow melting range enables clean melt viscosity reduction during oven bake cycles without volatilizing or generating smoky oven emissions.

4. Specifications & Quality Assurance

Uncontrolled residual unsaturation in Castor Wax leads to yellowing and premature softening. Nagdev Products maintains complete catalytic hydrogenation control with iodine values strictly below 3.0 g I₂/100g, ensuring maximum hardness, high dropping point, and thermal color stability.

Frequently Asked Technical Questions

What is the optimum activation temperature for HCO in epoxy coatings? +

For aromatic solvents (xylene, Solvesso), the optimal activation window is typically 50°C to 55°C. For aliphatic solvents, 55°C to 65°C is required. Overheating above 70°C dissolves the wax completely, which will seed or crystallize into coarse particles upon cooling.

How does Castor Wax compare to paraffin or PE waxes in PVC? +

Unlike petroleum paraffin waxes, HCO contains polar secondary hydroxyl groups that offer better polymer compatibility, reducing exudation and surface blooming while delivering superior gloss retention.

What is the HS code for Hydrogenated Castor Oil? +

Hydrogenated Castor Oil (Castor Wax) is classified under HS 1516.20 (indicative classification; final HS code confirmed at the time of export).

Can custom particle sizes be supplied for powder coatings? +

Yes, our processing lines can accommodate custom flake thicknesses or micronized particle distributions upon technical review for commercial volume contracts.
