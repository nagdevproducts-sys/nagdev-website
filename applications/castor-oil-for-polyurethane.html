Technical Application Guide

Bio-Polyurethane Polyols from Castor Oil Chemistry

Castor oil is nature's only commercially viable, naturally occurring trifunctional polyol. Containing three secondary hydroxyl groups per triglyceride molecule (hydroxyl value ~160–168 mg KOH/g), it offers polyurethane chemists a renewable, hydrophobic bio-polyol building block that imparts excellent hydrolytic endurance, electrical insulation, and elastomeric flexibility.

1. Trifunctional Architecture & Crosslink Density

Polyurethane networks are synthesized by reacting polyols with diisocyanates (such as MDI or TDI). Most vegetable oils lack hydroxyl functionality and require complex chemical epoxidation and ring-opening. Castor oil, in contrast, naturally possesses an average of 2.7 to 2.9 reactive hydroxyl groups per molecule.

When reacted with polymeric MDI, the natural trifunctionality yields a crosslinked polyurethane network with a tailored crosslink density. The long, flexible C18 hydrocarbon backbone acts as an internal flexibilizer, imparting high impact strength and low glass transition temperatures (Tg) without requiring external petroleum-based phthalate plasticizers.

2. Hydrophobic Resistance & Hydrolytic Stability in CASE Systems

Petroleum-derived polyether and polyester polyols are prone to hydrolytic cleavage or moisture absorption in humid environments. The long aliphatic hydrocarbon chains surrounding the urethane linkages in castor-based polymers create a pronounced hydrophobic shielding effect.

This makes castor oil the ideal polyol choice for:

  • Electronic Potting & Encapsulation Compounds: Outstanding dielectric strength, low moisture absorption, and thermal endurance protect sensitive printed circuit boards and transformer coils.
  • Industrial Flooring & Joint Sealants: Two-component polyurethane self-leveling floors exhibit exceptional resistance to chemical spills, washdown detergents, and thermal shock.
  • Corrosion-Resistant Pipeline Coatings: 100% solids, solvent-free polyurethane coatings applied to buried pipelines and marine pilings deliver decades of cathodic disbondment resistance.

3. Modified Bio-Polyols for Tailored Flexibility and Hardness

By chemical transesterification with polyhydric alcohols (glycerol, pentaerythritol) or partial polymerization, Nagdev Products develops specialized Bio-Polyurethane Polyols with hydroxyl values ranging from 110 to 350 mg KOH/g, providing system formulators precise control over Shore hardness (from Shore 40A elastomers to Shore 85D rigid plastics).

4. Moisture Control: Critical Factor in Urethane Synthesis

In isocyanate chemistry, trace moisture reacts with NCO groups to produce carbon dioxide gas and urea linkages, causing uncontrolled foaming and pinholes in solid coatings. Nagdev Products utilizes vacuum-drying systems to guarantee moisture contents below 0.05%–0.10% across all technical polyol grades.

Frequently Asked Technical Questions

What is the typical hydroxyl equivalent weight (HEW) of standard castor oil? +

Standard refined castor oil (FSG) with a hydroxyl value of ~163 mg KOH/g has a hydroxyl equivalent weight of approximately 344 g/eq (calculated as 56,100 / OH value).

Why is moisture control critical when using castor oil in polyurethanes? +

Water reacts aggressively with isocyanate groups (consuming 1 mole of water per 2 NCO groups), releasing CO2 gas which creates voids, bubbles, and pinholes. We supply vacuum-dehydrated castor oil with moisture under 0.08% to ensure bubble-free casting.

Can castor-based polyurethanes replace petroleum polyols in structural adhesives? +

Yes. Castor-based polyols are widely used in structural two-part polyurethane adhesives for bonding fiberglass, plastics, concrete, and metals due to their high peel strength and resistance to water immersion.

What export packaging formats are available for bulk polyurethane blenders? +

Supplied in 1,000L IBC totes, 200 kg steel drums with nitrogen blanketing, and 20–24 MT bulk ISO tanks / flexitanks.
